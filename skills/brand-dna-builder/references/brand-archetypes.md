# Brand Archetypes Reference

The 12 Jungian archetypes as applied to brand identity. Use this to identify and justify archetype classification in brand analyses. Most brands blend 2 archetypes — a primary and a secondary. When in doubt, choose the one that governs their communication style.

---

## THE INNOCENT
**Core desire:** Safety, happiness, simplicity
**Communication style:** Optimistic, honest, wholesome — never complicated or edgy
**Visual language:** Clean, bright, simple. Primary colors. Lots of white space.
**Brand examples:** Dove, Patagonia (nature messaging), Honest Company
**When to assign:** Brand leads with purity, authenticity, "back to basics" — avoids anything dark or controversial

---

## THE EVERYMAN (Regular Guy/Gal)
**Core desire:** Belonging, connection, fitting in
**Communication style:** Unpretentious, friendly, relatable — speaks like a neighbor, not an expert
**Visual language:** Warm, human, approachable. Real people over models. Nothing aspirational or exclusive.
**Brand examples:** IKEA, Target, BrewDog (early), Carhartt
**When to assign:** Brand emphasizes accessibility, community, anti-elitism, "we're just like you"

---

## THE HERO
**Core desire:** Mastery, achievement, proving worth through challenge
**Communication style:** Bold, motivational, performance-oriented — challenges the audience to do more
**Visual language:** Strong contrasts, dynamic imagery, action shots, athletic aesthetics
**Brand examples:** Nike, Under Armour, CrossFit, Red Bull
**When to assign:** Brand positions itself as the tool or partner in the customer's transformation/achievement story

---

## THE OUTLAW (Rebel)
**Core desire:** Revolution, disruption, freedom from rules
**Communication style:** Confrontational, irreverent, anti-establishment — explicitly against the category norms
**Visual language:** Dark, raw, distressed, asymmetric. Rules are broken deliberately.
**Brand examples:** Harley-Davidson, Death Wish Coffee, Cards Against Humanity, Liquid Death
**When to assign:** Brand defines itself by what it's against; actively positions against category conventions

---

## THE EXPLORER
**Core desire:** Freedom, discovery, new experiences, authentic self
**Communication style:** Independent, adventurous, restless — never content with the ordinary
**Visual language:** Expansive, outdoor, unfiltered. Natural light, wide shots, rough edges.
**Brand examples:** REI, Jeep, Arc'teryx, Allbirds (early)
**When to assign:** Brand celebrates independence, exploration, doing things your own way — freedom as a core value

---

## THE CREATOR
**Core desire:** Creation, self-expression, making something of enduring value
**Communication style:** Artistic, perfectionist, visionary — obsessed with craft and originality
**Visual language:** Distinctive, editorial, often asymmetric. Strong art direction. Celebrates making.
**Brand examples:** Apple (early Jobs era), Adobe, Moleskine, LEGO, Supreme
**When to assign:** Brand's identity centers on creation, craft, originality, or enabling the customer's own creative output

---

## THE RULER
**Core desire:** Control, order, prosperity, leadership
**Communication style:** Authoritative, confident, premium — positions itself as the standard
**Visual language:** Clean, expensive, structured. Classic typography. Gold, navy, black. No clutter.
**Brand examples:** Mercedes-Benz, Rolex, American Express Black Card, McKinsey
**When to assign:** Brand signals status, authority, and premium positioning — the customer aspires to be associated with it

---

## THE MAGICIAN
**Core desire:** Transformation, making dreams real, creating something from nothing
**Communication style:** Visionary, transformative, slightly mystical — sells the outcome, not the mechanism
**Visual language:** Otherworldly, gradient-heavy, luminous. Before/after energy. The transformation is the product.
**Brand examples:** Disney, Tesla, Apple (product launches), Dyson
**When to assign:** Brand's core promise is transformation — the customer believes the brand can make the impossible possible

---

## THE LOVER
**Core desire:** Intimacy, passion, sensory pleasure, beauty
**Communication style:** Sensual, intimate, indulgent — speaks to desire and pleasure, not utility
**Visual language:** Rich colors, close crops, texture-heavy, warm light. Beauty-first.
**Brand examples:** Chanel, Victoria's Secret, Aesop, Jo Malone, Cadbury
**When to assign:** Brand sells a sensory or emotional experience; beauty, indulgence, and pleasure are core to the positioning

---

## THE CAREGIVER
**Core desire:** Protection, nurturing, doing for others
**Communication style:** Warm, reassuring, service-oriented — the brand exists to take care of you
**Visual language:** Soft, warm, human. Real people. Physical comfort cues.
**Brand examples:** Johnson & Johnson, TOMS, Whole Foods, most healthcare brands
**When to assign:** Brand centers on care, protection, helping others — customer or community wellbeing is the explicit mission

---

## THE JESTER
**Core desire:** Joy, levity, living in the moment
**Communication style:** Playful, irreverent, funny — refuses to take itself seriously
**Visual language:** Bold colors, unexpected combinations, expressive typography. Anti-corporate.
**Brand examples:** Dollar Shave Club, Old Spice, Wendy's (social), Ben & Jerry's
**When to assign:** Brand's primary differentiator is humor, fun, or refusing the seriousness of its category

---

## THE SAGE
**Core desire:** Understanding, knowledge, truth
**Communication style:** Authoritative, analytical, educational — shares wisdom without selling it
**Visual language:** Clean, restrained, academic. Typography-led. Dense information presented beautifully.
**Brand examples:** TED, The Economist, Google (early), MIT Press, Basecamp
**When to assign:** Brand positions itself as a trusted source of knowledge; content and education are central to the identity

---

## Dual Archetype Patterns (Common Combinations)

| Primary | Secondary | Example Blend |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Explorer | Performance brand with freedom narrative |
| Outlaw | Jester | Disruptive brand that doesn't take itself seriously |
| Sage | Ruler | Premium authority brand with educational content |
| Creator | Magician | Craft brand with transformation as core promise |
| Caregiver | Everyman | Accessible, community-oriented service brand |
| Lover | Ruler | Luxury brand with sensory and status signals |

---

## Assignment Rules

1. **Observe, don't project.** Assign based on how the brand actually communicates, not what their About page claims.
2. **Copy is more reliable than visual identity.** Visuals get redesigned; the underlying voice usually doesn't.
3. **The villain tells you a lot.** Who or what is the brand implicitly against? That usually points to the archetype.
4. **When two fit equally, the archetype that governs the voice (not the visuals) is primary.**
